# Cannon Blastoise and mobile Octillery

Blastoise now illustrates the native 3-elixir Cannon. The existing original `dist/assets/cards/blastoise-mobile-v1.png` scene is retained: its two shell cannons, complete heavy body and natural riverbank remain recognizable. The active artwork key is `blastoiseCannon`; there is no active `CARD_ART.blastoise` entry. Its arena sprite stands on the ground without a platform. Native Shoot frame 2 contains the actual shell-cannon flash, so that frame is aligned to the unchanged release time. This visual contact selection does not change damage, cadence, first attack, lifetime, targeting or range.

The former mobile Blastoise role uses Octillery, species 224, under `octillery`. Its new scene is `dist/assets/cards/octillery-mobile-v1.png` (SHA-256 `9959aa9e32f48f16bcbbae209ab3c1907fca79ce5e06e4056e7c43d6ec821fd8`). The original official anatomy reference is cached at `dist/assets/pokemon/224/art.png`. Octillery's tube mouth emits water, its tentacles contact the stream bed, and the whole scene shares lighting and perspective. The illustration was generated with the built-in ImageGen tool, reviewed at full size, and approved by the supervisor for review in the actual card. No fallback API or local image editing was used. Two optional edits to the existing Blastoise scene were rejected by ImageGen; that scene was retained rather than replaced with a placeholder.

Octillery's unmodified native sheets, shadows, attachment markers, AnimData and credits come from PMDCollab/SpriteCollab commit `576a7f13b5b7f6ad09a5877d5ae6b2d33458c245`, folder `sprite/0224`. `credits.txt` records CHUNSOFT. The new species scale is 1.9231: its maximum opaque Idle height is 26 native pixels across the eight directions, producing the same 50-pixel visual target as the former mobile Blastoise. Collision radii remain catalog data. `sprite-geometry.js` adds 224 without changing any of the previous 103 species' geometry. Shoot uses its own native hit frame 2 and head marker at the mouth, not Blastoise's cannon markers. Main owns the Pokémon base stats, cries, catalog, deck migration and balance reference.

Browser evidence is in `.supervision/mobile-board-20261114/art-completion` at the shared workspace root, including the immutable Main and Art source SHAs and per-file hashes. The native pose sheets cover eight directions at six attack phases. Controlled real Game samples run at 60 simulation steps per second and record actual shots and positions; they do not constitute a subjective approval of all animations or a device performance certification. Camera, terrain and fields are checked at 390×700, 390×844, 768×1024 and 1440×900. The public release remains Main's responsibility.

The final Octillery prompt was:

> Create a polished original full-bleed portrait card illustration for the Pokémon game Poké Arena, matching a clean hand-painted anime adventure card scene. No lettering, no numbers, no card frame, no interface, no logos. Portrait aspect 4:5. One unmistakably faithful official-design Octillery Pokémon, a red octopus with a round domed head, a prominent short tubular cannon-like mouth with dark oval opening, two half-lidded white eyes with black pupils, small yellow bumps on top of the head and yellow circular suction pads on the curling red tentacles. Eight anatomically coherent tentacles, several partly occluded naturally; never give it ears, a shell, clothing, guns, extra heads or a fish body. This is a sturdy mobile ranged Pokémon, not a building. Octillery occupies the middle 65% of the picture and braces on its tentacles in a shallow rocky stream, facing in a three-quarter diagonal toward the right, firing a concentrated narrow jet of bright water from its tubular mouth toward the upper right edge. The water starts precisely at its mouth. Give the water jet a readable trajectory but keep the Pokémon silhouette dominant and complete. A tranquil riverside forest with warm green grass, scattered rounded stones, distant mossy rock bank, soft blue sky reflections and a little water splash integrated with its feet. Shared daylight, perspective, and ground contact across the entire scene, soft painted depth in the background, crisp simple cel-shaded character anatomy, restrained texture and clear separation at small card size. Natural action scene, no isolated cutout, no generic gradient background, no grid or cells, no decorative platform.
